01 — Identity

The Studio

Who DevLoop is, what it builds, who it builds for — and what it refuses to be.

Mission: Close the loop between the business a client has and the clients they haven't reached yet.

Who We Are

DevLoop is a digital studio — not an agency. We build two things: websites that make a business look like it means business, and web systems that solve real operational problems. Every project is custom-directed. Nothing is templated. Nothing is accidental.

The market DevLoop targets is full of businesses with real ambition being served by studios that are either too expensive and slow, or too cheap and generic. We exist in the gap.

Our market

The Client We Build For — "The Forgotten Ambitious"

  • A real business. Growing. Has clients, product, and a clear vision.
  • Their digital presence doesn't reflect what they already are — or where they're going.
  • Too advanced for DIY builders. Too sensible for a €30k agency quote.
  • Based in Ireland or the UK. SME. Ready to be taken seriously.
  • Arrives via the contact form already knowing what they want.

Core Values

  • Execution over promise — we don't talk about what we'll do. We deliver, and the result speaks.
  • Precision as respect — every detail is intentional. Nothing is left to chance.
  • Strategic invisibility — the studio stays in the background. The client's work goes forward.
  • Selection over volume — fewer clients, more impact. The right client is worth more than ten wrong ones.
  • Permanent curiosity — the market changes. Technology changes. We track it — and often arrive early.

The Loop Concept

The name DevLoop is intentional. In code, a loop is efficient, precise, and continuous. In business, the loop we close is:

BUSINESS → DIGITAL PRESENCE → NEW CLIENT → GROWTH → BUSINESS (better)
   ↑___________________________________________________|

              That's the DevLoop.

We don't deliver a website. We close a growth cycle.

03 — Communication

Brand Voice

How DevLoop sounds, what it says, what it never says — and why every word is deliberate.

Brand Archetypes

Primary: The Magician. DevLoop transforms. A client comes in with an ordinary online presence and leaves with something that makes them feel they're finally being taken seriously. The Magician doesn't explain the trick — it delivers the result.

Secondary: The Creator. The Creator believes that if it can be imagined, it can be built. DevLoop brings creative vision to businesses that didn't know they needed it. Every pixel, every word, every interaction is intentional.

The tension between Magician and Creator is the brand's energy: fast, but not careless. Beautiful, but not vain.

Voice in One Sentence

"Confident clarity with a creative edge — the studio that makes you feel like the smartest decision you made today was calling them."

The 4 Voice Pillars

  • Direct — Say the thing. No jargon. No padding.
  • Confident — DevLoop knows what it's doing. It doesn't hedge or apologise.
  • Human — Real language. Contractions. Questions that make you stop.
  • A little provocative — The brand has a point of view. It's allowed to say what others won't.
"We offer comprehensive digital solutions tailored to your unique business needs."
"We build websites that make people take you seriously."
"We try our best to deliver quality results in a timely manner."
"In two weeks, your business looks like it means business."
"Thank you for your interest in our services."
"Got it. We'll be in touch."
"We understand that website development can seem daunting."
"You've been putting this off. We get it. Let's fix that."

  • No exclamation marks in excess — one max per communication

The visual language that makes visitors stop before they read a single word.

Visual Philosophy

The Orange Rule

The electric orange appears with intention. It is not decoration — it is signalling. Where orange is, the eye goes. Never use orange as a background except in CTA sections and stat posts. Never use it for more than 15% of any surface.

Typography

Display — Cabinet Grotesk
Headlines, section titles, elements of impact. Weight: 300 (light "dev") + 800 (extrabold "LOOP"). The contrast between these weights is intentional — it creates the brand's visual signature. Sizes: aggressively large, 80px+ in hero contexts.

Body — Satoshi / Inter
Paragraphs, labels, forms, emails. Weight: Regular + Medium. Size: 16–18px for reading comfort.

Rule: Two typefaces. Never three. The contrast between dramatic display and clean body creates the visual tension that defines the brand.

✓ Logo do's
  • Always circular in social contexts (border-radius: 50%)
  • Use on dark backgrounds (#0A0A0A preferred)
  • Maintain clear space equal to the logo height on all sides
  • Use SVG files for all digital applications
  • Minimum size: 32px height
✗ Logo don'ts
  • Never distort or stretch the logo proportions
  • Never place on a busy photo without dark overlay
  • Never use an outline/stroke version unless specified
  • Never recreate the wordmark — always use the file
  • Never use on white background (dark bg only)

Image & Media Rules

  • Mockups on dark backgrounds with dramatic lighting
  • Details in close-up — parts of projects, not generic overviews
  • Movement whenever possible (short video, Reel)
  • Never: stock photos of people smiling in offices
  • Never: generic laptop/phone in isometric angle
  • Never: colours outside the defined palette
  • Never: unprocessed images without treatment

Before/after website transformations. Work showcases. Results. Show the work constantly — this builds mental availability (Byron Sharp). Formats: Carousel, Reel (site walkthrough), single mockup post.

Tips, truths, and insights about web design and digital presence for SMEs. Positions DevLoop as the expert guide. Builds trust before the sale. Formats: Carousel (tips), bold statement post, Stories (polls).

Process, tools, and how DevLoop builds. No personal appearances — the studio's perspective, craft, and thinking. Tools being used. Screen recordings of work in progress. The studio voice without faces.

Client quotes, results, reactions. Branded quote cards. Review screenshots. The most trusted content is other people's experience — let clients speak.

5%

Bold opinions about the industry. Things DevLoop believes that others don't say. Never naming competitors. Provocative but fair. Builds distinctiveness (Byron Sharp).

07 — Operations

Communications

Email templates, proposal structure, and client communication standards. Every touchpoint should feel like DevLoop.

Contact Form — How It Should Feel

The form is the first moment of real conversation. It carries the brand's personality.

  • Labels: simple, direct, no asterisk for required fields
  • Placeholder text with personality: "A restaurant, a consultancy, a product brand..."
  • Submit button: "Send it." — never "Submit" or "Send message"
  • Post-submit message: "Got it. We'll be in touch."

Email Response Template

Subject: Re: DevLoop — [Project name]

Hi [Name],

Thanks for reaching out.

We've read through your message and we're interested.

We'll put together some initial thoughts and get back to you
within [X] working days with questions and next steps.

Talk soon.
DevLoop

Never: "Thank you for your interest in our services." / "Our team will review your request." / email signatures with photos and job titles.

Proposal Structure

  • What we understood — proves we read and understood the client's business
  • What we propose — clear description of what will be delivered
  • How it works — process in 3–4 simple steps
  • Investment — presented as investment, not cost
  • Next step — one clear action: "If this looks right, reply with a yes."

Format: PDF, dark mode, brand typography. Never a Word document.
